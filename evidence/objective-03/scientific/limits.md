# Scientific mathematics and bounds

[Português brasileiro](limits.pt-BR.md)

Scientific accepts the bounded Rust operation `evaluate`. Its expression grammar supports decimal literals, exponent notation, parentheses, `+ - * / ^ !`, adjacent factors such as `2pi` and `iπ`, the degree suffix and the Unicode mathematical operators shown in the editor. Exponentiation associates right to left and binds more strongly than a unary minus. The configured angle unit applies to bare forward-trigonometric inputs and to inverse-trigonometric results. The explicit degree suffix converts its argument to radians before evaluating a forward function. Arithmetic between explicitly annotated angles requires compatible dimensions. [Parser and normalization](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/syntax.rs) · [Native entry point](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific.rs)

| Area | Supported scope |
| --- | --- |
| Trigonometry | `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan` with degrees, radians and gradians. Selected rational multiples of π and inverse special values have exact or symbolic results. Exact π-multiple angle reduction precedes numerical evaluation. |
| Hyperbolic functions | `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh`, `asinh`, `acosh`, `atanh`. Real singularities and supported complex branches have distinct domain behavior. |
| Logarithms and powers | Natural `ln`, common `log`, `log2`, `log10` and `log(x,b)` for positive real `b ≠ 1`. Integer powers and selected exact rational roots preserve exact values. `exp`, `sqrt`, `cbrt`, `root(x,n)` and `pow(x,y)` support their defined domains. In real mode a negative base with a supported odd-denominator rational exponent can produce a real value. |
| Constants and complex values | `pi`, `tau`, `e` and complex-mode `i`. Rationals and Gaussian rationals preserve their exact representation. The engine supports selected symbolic multiples and elementary identities, including `sqrt(-1)=i` in complex mode and `exp(iπ)+1=0`. Complex logarithms and nonintegral powers use principal values. |
| Factorial and display | Factorial accepts exact nonnegative integers. `abs` and explicit `approx` are available. Scientific and engineering notation preserve exact finite decimals, and nonterminating exact fractions stay rational. Numeric complex results use Cartesian form. |

## Domain and resource policy

- Real mode rejects square roots of negative reals, logarithms at nonpositive reals, inverse trigonometric values outside their real intervals and tangent at exact poles. Division by zero, `0^0`, a root index of zero, `atanh(±1)` and complex `atan(±i)` report a domain error. A principal complex `acosh(x)` on the real segment from −1 to 1 uses the imaginary value `i*acos(x)`. [Inverse trig principal values](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.23) · [Inverse hyperbolic principal values](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.37)
- The parser accepts up to 4,096 UTF-8 bytes, 256 tokens and 40 nested parse levels. The evaluator limits AST depth to 64. A decimal literal has at most 256 mantissa digits and decimal exponent magnitude 2,048. Each exact rational component has a numerator and denominator limit of 13,000 bits. Factorial is limited to 512, integer powers to exponent magnitude 4,096, and explicit root indices to magnitude 64. [Parser limits](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/syntax.rs) · [Literal limits](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/decimal.rs) · [Math limits](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific.rs)
- A numerical component's absolute binary exponent is limited to 13,000. Direct numerical trig range reduction has a separate binary-exponent bound of 128. Numerical exponential and hyperbolic growth arguments have an absolute bound of 4,096. Exact π-multiple reduction can keep a larger mathematical angle inside the trigonometric domain. [Numerical guards](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/numeric.rs)
- A numerical result must agree at the requested display length under two working precisions. An unresolved exact input that rounds to a different domain boundary reports `no_convergence`, and exact comparisons identify inputs already outside the real domain. Approximate sums or differences with complete cancellation or loss of more than 128 scale bits report `no_convergence` unless the computation preserves an exact identity. The engine reports unsupported operations and resource limits explicitly. [Cancellation and boundary checks](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific.rs)

## Precision and dependency decisions

The configurable 8–80 digit setting is a significant-digit display target. Numerical evaluation starts with at least `ceil(3.322 × digits) + 192` working bits before Astro Float word-size rounding, then repeats with an extra 96 bits. Decimal views round ties to even from the computed finite binary value. Agreement is a stability check. Approximate proof records leave their certified error bound empty. Exact rational operations and the selected symbolic identities use their own classification. [Precision and conversion implementation](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/numeric.rs) · [Display conversion](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/decimal.rs)

The implementation uses the pure-Rust `astro-float-num` backend pinned to 0.3.6 without its random or serialization features, alongside the existing arbitrary-precision integer and rational crates. The wider `astro-float` package also exposes a procedural expression macro; DARS uses its own bounded parser. `num-complex` offers a generic Cartesian representation, with its transcendental implementation requiring the `Float` trait and a concrete backend. The existing `Complex64` alias would fix each component at 64 bits. DARS retains small real and imaginary pairs over its two numeric representations. Rug offers arbitrary-precision real and complex operations through GMP, MPFR and MPC. Its native dependency and distribution profile made pure Rust the preferred approach for the shared native and WASM path. [Pinned numeric backend](https://docs.rs/astro-float-num/0.3.6/astro_float_num/) · [Astro Float macro](https://docs.rs/astro-float/latest/astro_float/macro.expr.html) · [Num Complex](https://docs.rs/num-complex/0.4.6/num_complex/struct.Complex.html) · [Rug](https://docs.rs/rug/latest/rug/)

The 0.3.6 decimal exporter trapped in the wasm32 parity run. Scientific imports exact integers into Astro Float through binary radix conversion and exports the computed finite dyadic value through the binary path. Bounded integers generate the base-ten digits and perform the display rounding. [Platform-safe decimal conversion](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/scientific/decimal.rs)
