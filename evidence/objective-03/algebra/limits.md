# Algebra mathematics and bounds

[Português brasileiro](limits.pt-BR.md)

Algebra accepts rational coefficients and the variables `x`, `y` and `z`. The grammar covers integers, exact decimal and exponent literals, parentheses, signed expressions, `+ - * / ^` and implicit products such as `2x` and `x(y+1)`. Exponentiation associates to the right and binds more strongly than unary minus. The real domain is the default. The complex option extends the solution domain while input coefficients and substitution values remain rational. [Parser](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra/syntax.rs) · [Operation dispatch](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra.rs)

| Operation | Supported scope |
| --- | --- |
| Simplify and expand | Exact sparse polynomial arithmetic and rational reduction, including univariate polynomial GCD cancellation. Multivariate reduction extracts common monomials and can cancel a denominator that divides the numerator exactly. Expansion distributes products and collects equal powers. |
| Factor | Rational linear factors with multiplicity and scalar content for univariate polynomials. The proof method reports partial extraction when a residual of degree three or higher remains. Multivariate extraction covers common monomials and differences of two rational monomial squares and is also classified as partial. For example, `x^4+5*x^2+4` is returned as a residual and the partial method leaves room for further factorization. |
| Polynomial | Descending coefficients of a reduced univariate polynomial, with missing coefficients filled by zero. `z^3-z/2` returns `[1,0,-1/2,0]`. |
| Solve | One equation in one variable. Rational factor extraction runs first, and the remaining factor must be constant or quadratic. Real irrational and complex quadratic roots are exact symbolic values. Roots carry multiplicity `[m=n]`, with explicit outcomes for an empty solution set and an identity on its recorded domain. |
| Inequality | One real univariate relation using `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=` or `!=`, with Unicode equivalents. A sign chart uses rational poles and rational excluded points. Numerator roots may also come from one residual quadratic. Open and closed intervals, unions, isolated points and the empty set are supported. An irrational pole or exclusion returns `unsupported`. |
| System | Linear numerators after rational reduction, independently of the degrees of retained exclusions. Rows are separated by semicolons or newlines. Exact reduced row echelon form returns a unique solution, an incompatible outcome or an affine family with free parameters `t1`, `t2` and `t3`. |
| Substitute and verify | Attach rational bindings to the input with semicolons, as in `x^2+y; x=2`. Substitution can leave variables free. Verification needs every variable of the submitted equality or inequality, as in `x^2=4; x=-2`. A candidate on an excluded point returns `false`. |

## Domain and proof

Division records the condition that the divisor be nonzero before rational reduction. Nonpositive powers apply the same condition to their bases, consistent with the domain error for `0^0`. Simplifying `x/x` gives `1` with `x != 0`. Solving `(x^2-1)/(x-1)=2` gives `none`. The only candidate is excluded by the original denominator. The recorded normalized input retains the operations needed to reproduce these conditions. [Rational arithmetic and domain tracking](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra/polynomial.rs)

Rational candidate roots are checked by exact Horner evaluation. Symbolic quadratic candidates are checked by division by their monic minimal polynomial. The same checks identify roots excluded by denominators. The inequality sign chart compares critical points exactly and uses exact rational values within intervals. Dyadic brackets refine irrational endpoints to find an interior sample. Linear systems substitute the affine family into original exclusions and record nonconstant constraints on the free parameters. An exclusion that vanishes on the entire family yields `none`. [Root verification](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra/roots.rs) · [Sign chart](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra/inequality.rs) · [Exact row reduction](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/algebra/linear.rs)

Every successful result is exact or symbolic. The engine classifies rational solutions and verification booleans as exact, and represents radical solutions, interval sets and affine families symbolically. The proof has no numerical precision or error estimate. The parser has no host-code execution path and supports the finite expression grammar above. General functions, nonlinear systems, general multivariate cancellation and complete high-degree factorization are outside this scope. An equation with an unresolved factor above degree two returns `unsupported` instead of a potentially incomplete solution set.

## Resource policy and validation

- A request has at most 4,096 UTF-8 input bytes. Each expression or equation parser accepts up to 256 tokens and 32 nested parse levels. Numeric literals have at most 128 mantissa digits and a decimal exponent magnitude at most 256.
- Polynomial degree and integer exponent magnitude are limited to 24. Each polynomial contains at most 256 terms, each product examines at most 8,192 term pairs, and each coefficient is bounded to 4,096 bits per numerator or denominator. Each reduced expression retains up to 16 distinct exclusion polynomials.
- Systems accept at most 16 rows and three variables. For degree three or higher, rational-root enumeration is bounded by a primitive leading-coefficient and constant-term magnitude of 1,000,000 and 4,096 positive divisor pairs before sign checks. Sign-chart endpoint bracketing uses up to 512 binary places. Bounds report `limit` or, when an interior sample cannot be separated by those brackets, `no_convergence`.
- Rust unit tests exercise replay, retained conditions, roots, interval endpoints, family restrictions, substitutions and structured errors. The independent Python fixture suite compares results with SymPy polynomial, equation, set and matrix operations.
