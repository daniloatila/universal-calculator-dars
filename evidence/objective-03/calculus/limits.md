# Calculus mathematics and bounds

[Português brasileiro](limits.pt-BR.md)

Calculus uses a real-valued grammar over `x`, `y`, `z`, rational constants, parentheses, `+ - * / ^`, signed integer powers and implicit products. The supported functions are `sin`, `cos`, `exp`, `ln` and `abs`. Trigonometric arguments use radians. The selected variable defaults to `x`. The normalized proof preserves the expression's structure so replay retains original domain conditions. [Grammar and exact representation](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/ast.rs) · [Operation contract](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus.rs)

| Operation | Supported scope |
| --- | --- |
| Derivative | Symbolic sum, product, integer power and chain rules. Order 1–8, default 1. Differentiation with respect to a selected variable treats the other variables as constants. |
| Antiderivative | Linearity, constant factors, univariate polynomials and supported powers or functions of affine arguments with rational slope. The reciprocal primitive uses `ln(abs(u))`. The result appends `+C`, which can depend on variables held constant. Some inputs, including `exp(x^2)`, report that a symbolic primitive is unavailable. |
| Definite integral | A finite closed interval with rational lower and upper endpoints. The supported primitive is evaluated at the endpoints and subtracted in the given order. Exact rational interval checks must establish that every original domain condition holds throughout. A pole, excluded point or invalid logarithm on the closed interval prevents a result. |
| Limit | Rational-function leading terms at rational points and signed infinity. Selected finite analytic limits use local Taylor coefficients, including `sin(x)/x` and `(1-cos(x))/x^2` at zero. Selected direct elementary compositions use the limiting argument and direction. Left and right limits are separate at finite points. Two-sided mode requires both directions to exist and agree. Infinite outcomes are `inf` or `-inf`. Absent or different directional limits give `does_not_exist` where the engine can establish the outcome. |
| Series | Taylor polynomial and big-O remainder about a rational point, default zero. The 1–12 setting counts Taylor coefficient positions including the constant, default 6. Coefficients are obtained by truncated series arithmetic, with exact results for rational terms and symbolic constants when needed. The proof records convergence for all real inputs, an open radius for recognized affine singularities, or local convergence where a radius is unavailable. |
| Numerical integral | Adaptive Simpson quadrature on a finite closed interval in the selected variable. Guards must establish continuity and the admitted smooth pieces before integration. The requested absolute tolerance defaults to `1e-8` and is limited to `1e-12` through `1e-3`. Reversing endpoints reverses the result. |

## Domain and method

- Division and nonpositive powers retain a nonzero condition on the original operand. The original argument of a real logarithm must be positive. For example, the derivative of `x/x` is zero with `x != 0`. The derivative rule for `abs(u)` can retain `abs(u) != 0`; the quadrature grammar also requires variable-dependent absolute-value arguments to stay away from zero. [Domain checks](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/ast.rs) · [Symbolic rules and interval enclosure](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/symbolic.rs) · [Elementary derivatives](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.7) · [Trig derivatives](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.20)
- Rational leading terms at a finite point use the shifted numerator and denominator. At infinity they use each polynomial's degree and leading coefficient. The approach sign determines the sign of any pole. Elementary finite limits expand at most 12 coefficient positions when needed. If the available terms cannot establish a limit, the request reports `no_convergence` or `unsupported`. [Directional limits](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/limits.rs)
- The Taylor engine convolves truncated coefficient vectors and applies recurrence relations for the logarithm, exponential and trigonometric functions. The center must satisfy recorded domain conditions and the admitted local expansion rules. The convergence condition for `ln(1+x)` about zero is `|x| < 1`. For `1/(1+z)` about two it is `|z-2| < 3`. The big-O term describes the local order of the omitted terms. [Series arithmetic](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/series.rs) · [Log and exponential series](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.6) · [Trig series](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.19)
- Primitive rules operate over the recorded domain. For `1/x`, an integration constant can differ across the two connected components. Definite integrals use one closed interval and the fundamental theorem. General elementary integration, special-function primitives, improper integrals, complex calculus and expansions about nonanalytic centers are outside the supported scope. [Primitive implementation](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/symbolic.rs) · [Log and exponential integrals](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.10)

## Approximation and resource policy

Composite Simpson panels begin with 16 equal divisions. Exact rational interval enclosures refine those seeds when a variable-dependent trigonometric argument varies by more than one quarter radian, an exponential argument varies by more than one half, or a reciprocal or logarithmic argument varies in magnitude by more than a factor of two. These checks address aliasing and narrow features in the admitted classes. The allowed argument magnitudes are 1,024 radians for trigonometry, 80 for exponentials and 1,000,000,000,000 for reciprocal and logarithmic arguments. A shape whose enclosure cannot meet the criteria within the seed budget reports `unsupported`. [Sampling and shape checks](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/numeric.rs)

The running calculation uses binary64 with 53 significand bits. The proof precision field records 15 decimal digits of working precision. The requested tolerance is a control target for Simpson refinement with a propagated rounding-sensitivity estimate. The proof reports the observed absolute quadrature estimate from refined versus coarse panels, explicitly marked uncertified. The rigorous Simpson remainder would require control of a fourth derivative on every panel. The certified error bound remains empty. Failure to reach the target within the budgets reports `no_convergence`. [Simpson estimate implementation](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/numeric.rs) · [Simpson remainder](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.5#ii)

- Requests accept 4,096 UTF-8 bytes, 256 tokens and up to 32 nested parse levels. Literals allow 128 mantissa digits and decimal exponent magnitude 256. Rational options use up to 256 bytes each. Input exponent magnitude is limited to 32, generated expression powers to 64, and normalized expression nodes to 2,048. Rational numerators and denominators are bounded to 4,096 bits. [Resource guards](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/ast.rs)
- The rational limit and polynomial primitive path has degree at most 64. An expression keeps up to 16 domain guards and closed-interval certification uses up to 2,048 checks and 12 subdivision levels. Numerical quadrature limits endpoint magnitude to 1,000,000, seed subdivision to 4,096 visits and 10 levels, and Simpson refinement to 32,768 samples and 16 levels. [Rational polynomial limits](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/limits.rs) · [Domain work bounds](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/symbolic.rs) · [Quadrature work bounds](../../../crates/dars-universal/src/calculus/numeric.rs)

The symbolic path uses the existing locked Rust arbitrary-precision rational crates. Direct symbolic differentiation supports a reusable expression tree and gives exact derivatives and Taylor coefficients where the grammar allows them. Finite differences would introduce step-size and cancellation errors for these operations. Adaptive Simpson uses sample reuse and a small native and WASM implementation. Gaussian and tanh-sinh quadrature were considered as broader alternatives. SymPy provides the independent symbolic oracle, and mpmath provides the higher-precision numerical reference. The independent fixtures check derivatives, candidate primitives by redifferentiation, exact integrals, directional limits, Taylor coefficients, adaptive integration and guard or budget errors.
